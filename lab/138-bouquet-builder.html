The rules of thumb

  • Odd numbers. Groups of three, five or seven read as natural and keep the eye moving; even groups tend to pair off and look staged. Every flower here comes in an odd count. It's a convention, not a law.
  • The golden ratio for height. A common guide is that the arrangement should stand about 1.5 to 2 times the height of its vase. This builder uses φ ≈ 1.618 exactly: the tallest bloom sits 1.618 vase-heights above the rim.
  • Colour wheel schemes. Monochrome keeps one hue and varies its lightness; analogous uses neighbours 30° either side; complementary pairs hues 180° apart; triadic spaces three hues 120° apart. Greenery stays green and isn't counted in the scheme.
  • Roles. The focal flower is the largest and draws the eye first; secondary flowers support it; fillers and greenery soften the gaps.
