Space City Transfer

Houston is nicknamed Space City for its long role in human spaceflight. This console plans the classic two-burn orbit change: from one circular orbit to another, around Earth or between planets around the Sun. It assumes circular, coplanar orbits and instant burns, the textbook idealisation.

White is the spacecraft on the transfer ellipse (amber), pink is where it is headed. Launch at the phase angle and the two meet on the far side, half an orbit later. Motion follows Kepler's equation, so the craft slows as it climbs.

Bi-elliptic comparison

A bi-elliptic transfer takes three burns: out past the target to an intermediate radius rb, then back down. It takes far longer, but when the orbits are far enough apart it can cost less. With rb taken to infinity, it wins once the radius ratio passes 11.94; above about 15.58 it wins for any rb beyond the outer orbit.
