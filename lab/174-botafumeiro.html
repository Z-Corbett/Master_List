Why pulling makes it climb

The censer is a pendulum whose rope length r you change. Its angle obeys

θ″ = −(g / r) sin θ − 2 (r′ / r) θ′ − c θ′

The middle term is the pump. Pulling in at the bottom, where the censer is fastest, works against the centrifugal pull and adds energy, much as a child on a swing stands up at the bottom. Letting the rope out at the ends, where it barely moves, costs almost nothing. Do the opposite and you take energy out, which is exactly how you bring it down.

The physicist Juan Sanmartín worked this out for the Santiago censer in 1984 and called it parametric pumping in the Middle Ages. This page simplifies it: a point mass, a rope that never goes slack below 88°, your pull speed capped at 2.5 m/s, and a little air drag.
