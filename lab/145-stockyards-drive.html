A little game inspired by Fort Worth's cattle drives

Stockyards Drive

In the Fort Worth Stockyards, a small herd of Texas longhorns is walked down the brick street as a nod to the city's cattle-trail past. This is a make-believe version: you're the cowhand at the back. The steers shy away from your horse and stick together like a flock, so ride steady, keep them off the side alleys, and ease them into the pens at the east end.

How the herd thinks

  • Separation: a steer steps away from any neighbour closer than a body length.
  • Alignment: it matches the average heading of the steers around it.
  • Cohesion: it drifts toward the middle of its neighbours.
  • Flight zone: inside about 120 px of your horse it moves away from you, harder the closer you are.

Push from straight behind and the herd flows; ride in too close and they split. A steer that reaches the back of an alley is a stray.

Scoring

+100 for each steer penned, −25 for each stray. Finish before the two-minute clock runs out and every whole second left is worth 5 more.
