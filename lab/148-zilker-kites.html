What the model does, and doesn't

The kite is a point on a straight 30 m line. Each step (1/120 s) it adds up lift and drag from the apparent wind, its weight, and the tail's drag; the line pulls back only when it's taut. The bridle fixes the sail's angle to the line, so as the kite climbs it flattens and the lift eases off: that's why it settles at an angle instead of flying overhead. Moving the bridle point toward the nose sets the sail more face-on (more pull); too far back and it flies flat and low. A small pitch wobble, stirred by the gusts, is damped by the tail: the longer the tail, the stronger the damping, at the price of a little extra drag.

  • Two dimensions only: no side-to-side swoops, loops or spins.
  • Flat-plate lift and drag curves, with no real stall.
  • The line is straight and weightless, with no sag.
  • The wobble is a damped spring with made-up stiffness; the tail's damping just grows with its length.
  • The wind is the same at every height, and the gusts are a seeded sum of sine waves.

So it shows the right trends, not real numbers for a real kite.
