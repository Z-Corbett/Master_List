Weigh out a cold-process soap recipe (lye, superfat, water and fragrance) from SAP values checked against four published soap-making charts, or work out the wax and fragrance for a container candle. Everything is worked out by weight, in your browser. This is a planning aid, not a guarantee that a recipe is safe.

Lye is caustic: read before you start

  • Always add lye to water, never water to lye. Pouring water onto lye can boil and spatter violently.
  • Wear splash goggles and chemical-resistant gloves, with long sleeves. Mix in a well-ventilated place and don't breathe the fumes.
  • Keep children and pets away. Label lye and raw soap batter clearly.
  • Never use aluminium: lye reacts with it. Use stainless steel, heat-safe PP#5 plastic or glass rated for the heat.
  • Weigh everything, lye, water and oils, by weight on a digital scale. Never measure by volume.

    Where the SAP values come from

    The NaOH figures used are SoapCalc's (coconut is its 76 °F grade). Each one was checked against three more charts, and the range across all four is shown below; a single oil's SAP value naturally varies by a few percent. KOH figures are derived as NaOH × 56.1/40 (molar masses of KOH and NaOH), not read from a chart. A larger spread, like coconut's, is a reason to keep a superfat.
