    How the preview measures, and what it can't know

    Width, not characters. Google truncates titles and descriptions by the space they take up, not by a character count. About 580–600 px is the commonly cited width for a desktop title, which is why "60 characters" is only a rule of thumb: sixty narrow letters fit where forty wide ones don't. This page measures text with canvas.measureText in an Arial-compatible system font and cuts at a word boundary with an ellipsis. The layout assumptions are:

    PreviewTitleDescription
    Desktop1 line × 600 px at 20 px2 lines × 600 px at 14 px
    Mobile2 lines × 316 px at 18 px3 lines × 316 px at 14 px
    • These are approximations. Real results depend on the device, the font, the query and the layout of the day, and Google often rewrites titles (drawing on the title element, headings, og:title and anchor text) and picks its own snippet from the page text.
    • A title that repeats the h1 exactly is not an error, and Google has not called it a problem. It is flagged as a note because the title is often the better place for extra context, such as the brand.
    • Open Graph: 1.91:1 (for example 1200 × 630) is the widely used size for large link previews. X documents 2:1 for summary_large_image, so the check accepts roughly 1.8:1 to 2.05:1. X falls back to og:title, og:description and og:image when its own tags are missing, but twitter:card itself has no fallback.
    • Google has said the meta keywords tag is ignored, so it isn't checked.

    Sources: Google Search Central, "Influencing your title links in search results" and "Control your snippets in search results" · ogp.me · developer.x.com "Cards markup" and "Summary card with large image"
