Plucked String

A burst of noise goes round a short delay line. Each trip it is averaged with its neighbour and loses a little energy, so the hiss settles into a pitched, decaying tone. That is the whole Karplus–Strong algorithm (1983), computed here sample by sample in JavaScript.

How it works

For a string at f Hz the delay line holds N = round(sampleRate / f − ½) samples, filled with seeded white noise that the brightness control smooths with a one-pole lowpass. Every output sample is fed back as y[n] = ρ · ½ (y[n−N] + y[n−N−1]). The two-point average is a lowpass that also adds half a sample of delay, so the loop is N + ½ samples long and the pitch is sampleRate / (N + ½). Rounding N to a whole sample leaves a small tuning error, shown in cents under the spectrum.

Higher partials are cut more on every trip, so the tone darkens as it fades. The fundamental loses a factor of ρ·cos(π f₀ / sampleRate) per period, which gives the decay time below.

Equal temperament from A4 = 440 Hz: key n of an 88-key piano sounds 440 · 2^((n − 49)/12) Hz.
