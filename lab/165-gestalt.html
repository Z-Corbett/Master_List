Gestalt

how we group what we see

Before you read a single word on a page, your eye has already sorted it into clumps: this goes with that, those belong apart. The Gestalt psychologists of the early twentieth century described the habits behind that sorting. Designers lean on them every day, mostly without naming them.

Each toy below has one slider that makes a grouping appear or dissolve. Say how many groups you see, then compare with a deliberately simple computer model. Where you disagree with it is the interesting part.

The principles come from Gestalt psychology (Max Wertheimer, Wolfgang Köhler, Kurt Koffka and later researchers; common region was described by Stephen Palmer in 1992). All explanations, toys and models here are original simplifications, not published models of perception.
