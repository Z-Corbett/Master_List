How the colours mix

Real fibre-reactive dyes absorb light across the whole spectrum and bond with cotton through chemistry this page doesn't model. Here each dye is treated as a filter: the fabric's reflectance in each of the red, green and blue channels (in linear light) is multiplied by the dye's colour raised to the power of how much dye is there, like stacking tinted glass (a Beer–Lambert-style rule). So yellow over turquoise goes green and every extra dye makes the fabric darker, never lighter, the way dyes behave. Three RGB channels can't capture everything, though: some real mixes come out muddier or brighter than this.
