    How it's computed

    k ≈ (year − 2000) × 12.3685  (integer: new, +¼ first quarter, +½ full, +¾ last quarter)
    JDE = 2451550.09766 + 29.530588861 k + 0.00015437 T² − …,  T = k / 1236.85
    + periodic terms in M, M′, F, Ω (25 for new/full, 25 + W for quarters) + 14 planetary arguments A₁…A₁₄

    Meeus's times are in Terrestrial Time; the page subtracts ΔT (about 69 s in the 2020s, interpolated from a table, approximate outside 1900–2025) to get UTC. The check value from Meeus's example 49.a, the new moon of 1977 February 18, comes out as JDE – (book: 2443192.65118). The instantaneous phase angle uses the short series from chapter 48, good to a few hundredths of a degree for this purpose. Illuminated fraction k = (1 + cos i) / 2.

    Not the Easter moon

    The full moon that sets the date of Easter is the ecclesiastical (Paschal) full moon. It comes from the Church's tables built on the 19-year Metonic cycle and a fixed 21 March equinox, not from the sky. It usually falls within a day or two of the astronomical full moon shown here, but it can differ, and in some years that moves Easter by a week or even a month compared with a purely astronomical rule.
