Easter Dates

Easter is the first Sunday after the ecclesiastical full moon on or after March 21. The calendar arithmetic that turns that rule into a date is the computus. Here it is worked out for any year from 1583 to 4099, step by step.

    The Orthodox churches still reckon Easter on the Julian calendar. The Julian date is converted to the Gregorian calendar through the Julian Day Number; in this century the calendars are 13 days apart, and from March 1, 2100 they are 14 apart.

    How often each date comes round

    Western Easter can fall no earlier than March 22 and no later than April 25. The Gregorian pattern only repeats every 5,700,000 years, far too long to chart, so this uses 1583–4099, the range the algorithm is usually quoted for. Orthodox Easter, shown in Gregorian dates, drifts later as the calendars diverge.
