Type Scale & Pairing

Pick a base size and a ratio, and every other size follows by multiplying: base × ration. Then make it fluid, check that it reads, and take the CSS with you.

1 Scale

Sizes are exact products; the table shows px to 2 decimals, and CSS is written in rem (px ÷ 16) to 4 decimals.

2 Fluid sizes

At the min viewport each step is base × ration; at the max viewport it is base-at-max × ratio-at-maxn; in between it grows in a straight line, and it stops growing outside that range.

3 Colour and contrast

—

Contrast is the WCAG 2 ratio (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05) of relative luminance. AA asks 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text (24 px, or 18.66 px bold); AAA asks 7:1 and 4.5:1.

Line-height guidance: 1.5 for body text, 0.1 less per step up, never below 1.1 or above 1.6 (1.5 − 0.1 × step, clamped). Big type needs less leading; small type needs a little more.

5 Measure

Reading is a rhythm of short hops. The eye jumps along a line, takes in a few words at a time, then has to find the start of the next line. When lines run too long, that return trip gets lost and readers skip or repeat a line; when they are too short, the eye is forever turning back and sentences break into fragments. Somewhere in between, usually around forty-five to seventy-five characters for a single column of body text, the page settles into an easy pace. Treat that range as a guideline rather than a law: a narrow sidebar or a wide display line will sit happily outside it, and larger type or looser leading can carry a slightly longer line.
