About Talavera

Talavera poblana is tin-glazed earthenware made in the Mexican states of Puebla and Tlaxcala since colonial times. It grew out of Spanish majolica, named after Talavera de la Reina in Spain, and absorbed Moorish, Italian and Chinese influences along the way. Pieces are thrown or moulded by hand and painted by hand over a milky white glaze, with cobalt blue as the classic colour.

The name is protected: genuine Talavera comes from certified workshops in the region and follows traditional methods and a short list of colours. This page is a digital homage to the look, not an imitation of any workshop's designs.

The symmetry names come from crystallography. There are exactly 17 wallpaper groups; p4m, p4g, p2mm and p6m are four of them, and tile makers were using all four long before they had names.
