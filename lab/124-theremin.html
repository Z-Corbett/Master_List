Theremin

Léon Theremin's instrument is played without touching it: one hand sets the pitch, the other the volume. Here the field stands in for the air between the antennas. Left to right is pitch, bottom to top is volume.

Press and drag in the field to play; lift to stop. Keyboard: Space or Enter sounds the note, ← → move pitch, ↑ ↓ volume, Shift for bigger steps, Home/End jump to the ends, Esc stops.

The mapping

f = f₀ · 2^(octaves · x),  x ∈ [0, 1] left → right
gain = 1 − y,  y ∈ [0, 1] top → bottom
MIDI n = 69 + 12 · log₂(f / 440),  cents = 100 · (n − round(n))

Pitch is exponential in x because we hear ratios, not differences: equal steps across the field are equal musical intervals. Snapping picks the nearest allowed note by pitch (in cents), then plays its exact 12-tone equal-tempered frequency, 440 · 2^((n − 69)/12).

Scales

Chromatic: all 12 notes. Major: 0 2 4 5 7 9 11 semitones above the key. Major pentatonic: 0 2 4 7 9. Blues: 0 3 5 6 7 10 (minor pentatonic with the flat fifth). The real instrument has no frets at all, which is why theremin players practise pitch by ear; switch snapping off to hear that.
