How the water moves

  • Water falls, slides off slopes and spreads sideways up to three cells a tick, so it always finds the lowest gap.
  • Sandbags drop straight down and stack into a wall. They are heavier than water, so they sink through it: build before the creek rises.
  • Debris (branches, trash, a shopping cart) clogs the culvert under the road. The shovel clears it and cuts limestone.
  • Limestone stays put. The bedrock and the concrete culvert can't be dug.
  • A house floods the moment water touches it. Water that reaches the lake, or spills over the weir, is safe.

Central Texas is called Flash Flood Alley because thin soils over limestone send heavy rain straight into the creeks. At a flooded low-water crossing the rule is simple: turn around, don't drown.
