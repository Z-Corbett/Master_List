Rows and changes

With n bells, a row is an ordering of all n. Ringers move only one place at a time, so a change swaps adjacent pairs. Place notation lists the places where bells stay put ("x" means every pair swaps), and a method is its place notation repeated lead after lead until rounds come back.

Handstroke and backstroke

Each bell swings a full circle and back, so rows alternate between handstroke (pulling the sally) and backstroke (the tail end of the rope). A small pause, the handstroke gap, comes before every handstroke. At peal speed a band strikes about four blows a second.

The blue line

Ringers learn a method by the path their bell traces through the rows, traditionally drawn in blue with the treble in red. In Plain Bob the treble plain-hunts while the other bells dodge and make places, and the plain course visits every lead once.
