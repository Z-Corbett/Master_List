Gold and pigment

Medieval illuminators laid gold leaf over a raised ground and burnished it until it shone like metal. Their blues came from ground lapis lazuli or azurite, their bright reds from vermilion, and their greens often from verdigris, a copper pigment that could eat into the parchment over the centuries.

Vines and acanthus

Decorated initials often sprout foliage: scrolling vines, curling acanthus leaves and small gold dots called bezants. The stems here are generated as seeded spirals and alternate over and under each other and the letter, the way painted interlace does.

Drolleries

Margins of later medieval manuscripts are full of drolleries: snails, hares, birds and odd hybrid creatures that have little to do with the text. These are drawn in code and perched on the vines. No real manuscript is copied.
