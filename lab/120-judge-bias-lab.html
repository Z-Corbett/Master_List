Judge Bias Lab: Alder vs Birch

When one language model grades another's answers side by side, it tends to favour whichever answer it reads first, and whichever is longer. This lab puts numbers on both, and on the usual fixes.

This is a simulation. No language model is called. "Alder" and "Birch" are invented systems, and every answer is reduced to a hidden quality score and a word count drawn from a seeded random generator. The "judge" is a formula: it prefers the better answer, plus a bonus for the first position, plus a bonus for length, plus noise. That formula is what lets the page show the ground truth.

Swapping cancels position bias exactly, because the bonus goes to Alder in one order and to Birch in the other. Length control removes the verbosity slope. Majority voting cuts the noise, but it can't remove a bias every judge shares.
