Field notes from the porch

The cricket thermometer

Amos Dolbear published his chirp-rate rule in 1897; it fits the snowy tree cricket best. Crickets are cold-blooded, so their chirping speeds up as the night warms. The synthesized crickets here chirp in unison, as snowy tree crickets tend to.

Moths aren't really "drawn in"

High-speed tracking studies suggest moths keep their backs turned toward the brightest light, which they normally read as the sky. A bulb traps them in endless orbits. Flip the porch light off and watch them drift off.

Fireflies prefer the dark

Firefly courtship runs on flash signals, and artificial light can drown them out. Near the bulb they go quiet. Warmer nights also speed up their flashing.

Heat lightning

It's ordinary lightning from a storm too far away for its thunder to reach you. The flashes light up the clouds on the horizon and you hear nothing.
