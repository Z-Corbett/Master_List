Every page is a dot and every internal link an arrow. The graph finds the pages nobody links to, counts the clicks from home to each page, catches links to pages that don't exist, and runs the original PageRank to show where link equity pools. Then it suggests a few links that bring the deepest pages closer to home.

        About the PageRank here

        PR(p) = (1 − d) / N + d · ( Σq → p PR(q) / out(q) + Σdangling q PR(q) / N ),  d = 0.85

        Dangling pages have no outgoing links, so in the raw formula their rank would drain out of the graph and the total would shrink every round. The standard fix, used here, treats a dangling page as linking to every page equally, so the ranks always sum to 1. Duplicate links between the same two pages count once, self-links are ignored, and links to missing pages are left out of the calculation (they're reported as broken).

        A teaching model. This is the 1998 formula from Brin and Page's paper, in its probability form (the ranks sum to 1 rather than to N). It is not Google's current ranking system, and nothing here predicts rankings. It shows how internal links spread importance, and which pages a site's own links treat as central.

        Depth. Google doesn't publish a click-depth limit. "Within three clicks of home" is a common rule of thumb for crawlability and for users, not a documented ranking rule.
