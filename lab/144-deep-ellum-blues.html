Deep Ellum Blues

12 bars · shuffle · blues scale

Deep Ellum, just east of downtown Dallas, was a centre of early blues. Blind Lemon Jefferson performed there in the 1920s. This page is a small tribute, not a history: it writes a 12-bar blues, a shuffle bass and a seeded solo, and plays them with synthesized Web Audio voices.

How it's built

The form. Twelve bars of dominant sevenths: I–I–I–I, IV–IV–I–I, V–IV–I–V. The last bar's V is the turnaround back to the top. A quick change moves to IV in bar 2 and back.

The shuffle. Each beat splits into a triplet, and the off-beat eighth falls on the third triplet: at 2/3 of the beat, a long–short 2:1 swing.

The solo. Notes come from the blues scale (minor pentatonic plus the ♭5: 1, ♭3, 4, ♭5, 5, ♭7). On the strong beats (1 and 3) the solo lands on a note that is also in the current chord. The same ?seed= plays the same solo.
