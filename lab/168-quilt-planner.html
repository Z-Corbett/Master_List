    Assumptions

    • Seam allowance ¼ in. Squares and rectangles are cut finished size + ½ in.
    • Half-square triangles, two at a time: cut squares finished + ⅞ in. The exact geometry is finished + ½ + ¼√2 ≈ +0.854 in; ⅞ is that rounded up to the ruler's ⅛, leaving a sliver to trim.
    • Flying geese, stitch-and-flip: goose rectangle (W + ½) × (H + ½), two sky squares (H + ½).
    • 42 in usable width of fabric (WOF) after selvages. Pieces are cut from strips across the width, strip width = the piece's short side, no strips shared between piece sizes.
    • Yardage is rounded up to the next ⅛ yd. Nothing extra is added for shrinkage, straightening or mistakes: many quilters add ¼ yd.
    • Quilt sizes are targets, not standards: Crib 36 × 52, Throw 60 × 72, Twin 70 × 90, Queen 90 × 108, King 108 × 108 in. The block count is the nearest whole number that fits inside the border.
    • Borders are butted: two sides cut to the centre height, then top and bottom across the full width, all cut from WOF strips joined with straight seams (½ in lost per join).
    • Binding: double-fold, strips of the chosen width (2½ in is common practice). Length = quilt perimeter + 12 in for the corners and the join; 40 in of each 42 in strip is counted as usable after the diagonal joins.
    • Backing: 4 in bigger than the quilt on every side, pieced from 42 in widths with ½ in seams (1 in lost per seam), run whichever way uses less fabric.
    • Greyscale: relative luminance Y = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B on linearised sRGB, shown as the sRGB grey of the same luminance. Value bands use CIE L*: light ≥ 65, dark < 40.
